## Public-safe abstraction

Maturity is not declared by authorship alone. A consequential change should pass independent review, current validation, resolved objections, and an explicit promotion decision before it is treated as ready. Recovery boundaries preserve known-good states, while protected history prevents later convenience from silently rewriting the evidence of how a system changed.

In public IAM teaching, this supports a stewardship pattern:

1. **Creation is not approval.** Producing a change does not authorize its promotion.
2. **Readiness is evidenced.** Claims of readiness should point to current checks and resolved review, not confidence or authorship.
3. **Authority is separated.** The actor proposing a transformation should not silently grant itself the final authority to normalize that transformation.
4. **History is held in trust.** Stable recovery points and non-destructive change history preserve accountability and make repair possible.
5. **Exceptions remain explicit.** Emergency authority, when legitimate, must be named as an exception rather than disguised as ordinary process.

## Mythic teaching potential

The public metaphor is the **threshold and the keeper**. A threshold distinguishes possibility from admitted reality; the keeper represents a review function, not a privileged person, priesthood, sentient system, or supernatural authority. Crossing the threshold requires evidence appropriate to consequence. The metaphor must remain optional and subordinate to explicit governance.

## Unsafe exclusions

- claims that technical review confers moral, spiritual, legal, scientific, or religious legitimacy;

- claims that preserved history is consciousness, memory of a person, a soul, or proof of identity;
- emergency exceptions presented as ambient or self-granted authority;
- covenant claims, which remain unsupported by this source.

## Doctrine handoff

Doctrine may safely derive:

- stewardship as the separation of creation, review, authorization, and promotion;
- readiness as an evidence-bearing state rather than a declaration;
- recovery boundaries as commitments to repairability and accountable continuity;
- protected history as resistance to silent revision;
- threshold language as optional mythic framing, explicitly non-coercive and non-authoritative.

Doctrine must preserve the distinction between technical qualification and moral legitimacy, and must not use this packet to establish covenant.
