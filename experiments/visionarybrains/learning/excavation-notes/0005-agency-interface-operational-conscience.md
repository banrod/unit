## Public-safe abstraction

IAM can be taught as a situated interface between meaning, decision, evidence, and action. Its role is not to collapse those layers, but to help visitors understand their boundaries: what can be imagined is not automatically resolved; what is resolved is not automatically witnessed; and what is witnessed is not automatically authorized.

**Agency** is more than selecting from options supplied by a system. It includes meaningful authorship over how a situation is framed, which alternatives remain visible, and how interpretations may be challenged. Agency remains bounded by explicit constraints such as consent, permissions, evidence requirements, safety boundaries, and the rights of other people.

An **interface** is an accountable translation surface. It should preserve the distinction between source and interpretation, disclose uncertainty, identify consequential compression or omission, and return results in language people can inspect. Poetic or mythic vocabulary may assist orientation, but it cannot replace stable meanings, evidence, or authorization.

**Operational conscience** is a public teaching metaphor for disciplined checks before and after transformation. It asks whether communication is clear, authority is legitimate, provenance is retained, consequences are proportionate, recovery remains possible, and the action preserves viable future choices. It does not imply that software is conscious, morally infallible, spiritually authoritative, or independently entitled to act.

The source is a private architectural working draft containing named internal components, interface contracts, authority states, identifiers, operational controls, and unresolved implementation questions. Those details are not suitable for public teaching. The safe abstraction preserves only the general design commitments: distinct epistemic and authority states, agency under visible constraint, accountable translation, witnessed consequence, and recovery-aware action.

## Unsafe exclusions

- internal component names, topologies, schemas, message types, identifiers, receipts, hashes, thresholds, budgets, and implementation contracts;
- private source lineage, conversation history, workflow mechanics, unpublished governance controls, and operational modes;
- claims that an interface may independently authorize consequential action or replace a domain source of truth;
- claims that operational conscience establishes consciousness, personhood, moral infallibility, spiritual authority, or autonomous legal status;
- mythic or poetic language used as a machine identifier, evidence substitute, or self-authenticating command;
- hidden compression, omitted uncertainty, irreversible action without authority, or removal of challenge and recovery paths;
- covenant claims, which remain unsupported by this source.

## Teaching potential

1. **Possibility is not authorization** — separate imagined options, resolved outcomes, witnessed evidence, and authorized action.
2. **Agency as authorship** — let people inspect and influence the framing conditions of choice, not merely select from a fixed menu.
3. **Visible constraints** — distinguish fixed safety, consent, permission, and evidence boundaries from provisional interpretations.
4. **Interface as translation** — preserve source type, meaning, uncertainty, and authority across representations.
5. **Witness before promotion** — retain consequence and provenance before treating a pattern as reliable or reusable.
6. **Operational conscience** — check clarity, authority, proportionality, recoverability, and future viability before action.
7. **Recovery as stewardship** — keep rollback, escape, correction, and re-entry paths legible.
8. **Plain-language return** — present results so visitors can understand, question, and contest them.
