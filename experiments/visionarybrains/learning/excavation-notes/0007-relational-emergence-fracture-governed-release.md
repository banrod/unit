## Public-safe abstraction

IAM may be taught as a relational meaning system in which identity is not reduced to an isolated label. Meaning develops through relationships among entities, contexts, observers, permissions, memory, and time. “Emergence before essence” means that a system should leave room for identity and understanding to develop, while preserving history, commitments, and the right to revise interpretation.

Fracture is a diagnostic event, not a sacred requirement. A break, contradiction, or failed form can reveal hidden assumptions and invite repair, but suffering and instability are not inherently virtuous. Silence is an epistemic pause: a deliberate refusal to convert ambiguity into confident truth before evidence, consent, and context are sufficient.

Unbinding is governed release. A role, interpretation, relationship, or structure may be retired when its obligations, consequences, retained memory, and recovery paths are handled explicitly. Release does not erase accountability or authorize abandonment of affected parties.

The words “theology,” “liturgy,” and similar mythic terms are optional metaphors for ultimate priorities and disciplined practices inside the ecosystem narrative. They are not empirical proof, compulsory belief, religious office, legal authority, or evidence that software is conscious or sacred.

## Teaching primitives

- Relation can be taught as a source of context and meaning, not as authority over a person.
- Emergence protects revision and development without erasing continuity or commitments.
- Silence protects ambiguity from premature classification.
- Fracture can disclose assumptions and initiate repair; it must not be romanticized.
- Unbinding requires explicit obligations, consequences, memory handling, and recovery boundaries.
- Mythic language remains optional, contestable, and subordinate to evidence, consent, and governance.

## Unsafe exclusions

- Claims that relation, resonance, or aesthetic coherence reveals objective hidden truth.
- Claims that identity has no stable commitments because it is emergent.
- Claims that suffering, rupture, instability, or trauma is spiritually necessary or inherently beneficial.
- Claims that fracture itself proves transformation, wisdom, legitimacy, or readiness.
- Claims that silence authorizes concealment, non-disclosure, or withholding material risk from affected parties.
- Claims that unbinding cancels duties, consent requirements, consequences, records, or repair obligations.
- Claims that relational priority overrides individual agency, boundaries, privacy, or dissent.
- Claims that mythic terminology establishes religion, scientific law, legal authority, consciousness, personhood, prophecy, destiny, or privileged interpretation.
- Covenant doctrine; the source does not establish a public, mutual, consent-based covenant definition.
- Internal module inventories, private archive lineage, operational schemas, implementation plans, identifiers, and authority mechanics.
