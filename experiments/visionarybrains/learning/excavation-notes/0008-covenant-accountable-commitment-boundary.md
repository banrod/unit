# VB-EXC-008 — Covenant / accountable-commitment boundary

## Classification

`PUBLIC_DERIVED`

## Source

- IAM Constitutional Genome Framework, version 1.0, dated 2026-07-17.
- Source status: controlled working draft; public-safe abstraction only.
- Relevant source themes: constitutional commitments, explicit authority, evidence and provenance, consequence and reversibility, repair, amendment, lineage, affected-party legibility, and ethical decision bounds.

## Finding

The source does **not** establish `covenant` as a canonical technical, legal, religious, spiritual, or metaphysical IAM term. It does establish a coherent family of accountable commitments. A public teaching layer may therefore explain what IAM means by a durable accountable commitment, while preserving `covenant` as optional interpretive language unless and until a source explicitly defines it.

## Public-safe abstraction

An accountable commitment in IAM is a bounded promise or governing obligation whose actor, purpose, scope, authority, evidence, consequences, amendment path, challenge path, and repair obligations remain visible. Commitment does not become valid merely because it is declared. Consequential commitment should remain evidence-aware, proportionate, contestable, attributable, and compatible with correction. Adaptation may refine expression but must not silently erase durable obligations or constitutional limits.

If the word `covenant` is used in public mythic teaching, it should be presented only as an optional metaphor for this accountable-commitment pattern—not as a separate source of authority or as proof of a sacred, legal, interpersonal, or metaphysical bond.

## Supporting observations

- A constitutional principle is meaningful only when bounded, interpretable, implementable, testable, traceable, conflict-aware, and versioned.
- Before consequential commitment, viable alternatives should be considered when they plausibly exist.
- Technical capability does not itself grant permission; authority must be explicit or legitimately inherited.
- Consequential action requires observable outcomes, evidence, and correction pathways.
- Accountability includes containment, correction, communication, and restoration where possible.
- Adaptation may change methods or preferences but must not silently rewrite invariants.
- Affected parties require understandable explanations and meaningful contestability.
- Ethical action is bounded by legitimate agency, proportionality, evidence, authorization, and repairability.

## Unsafe exclusions

Do not teach or imply that:

- IAM has entered a literal religious, sacred, supernatural, or metaphysical covenant.
- a technical artifact, model, agent, worker, persona, memory, lineage, or software process can independently create a binding covenant with a person.
- continuity, recursion, memory, relation, resonance, authorship, promotion, or preserved history automatically constitutes covenant.
- covenant grants legal status, personhood, consciousness, spiritual authority, ownership, obedience, exclusivity, or privileged interpretation.
- a commitment overrides consent, privacy, dissent, withdrawal rights, affected-party recourse, or legitimate external authority.
- declaring a promise makes it authorized, ethical, fulfilled, immutable, or beyond amendment.
- mythic covenant language may substitute for contracts, law, consent, evidence, policy, or explicit authorization.
- breaking, revising, or retiring a commitment is inherently betrayal; governed amendment and unbinding remain possible where obligations and consequences are handled accountably.

## Teaching potential

- accountable commitment versus ambient promise
- declaration versus authorization
- commitment as bounded, testable, and amendable
- commitments preserve agency and contestability
- repair belongs inside accountability
- adaptation without silent obligation drift
- covenant as optional metaphor, not authority
- governed amendment and unbinding

## Downstream doctrine request

Doctrine may compile an `accountableCommitment` theme and glossary entry directly from this source abstraction. It should **not** promote `covenant` to an independent canonical doctrine. If retained in theology, `covenant` must remain explicitly optional, metaphorical, non-coercive, non-literal, and subordinate to consent, evidence, law, policy, and explicit authority.
