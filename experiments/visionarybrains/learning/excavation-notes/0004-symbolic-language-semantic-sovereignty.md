## Public-safe abstraction

IAM's symbolic vocabulary can be taught as an optional language for noticing relationships, uncertainty, transition, memory, and accumulated pressure. A **field** is a bounded situation containing entities, relations, pressures, absences, and observer positions. A **ring** is a bounded traversal with entry, movement, return, closure, and residue. A **gravity well** is a metaphor for accumulated salience, dependency, inertia, or unresolved pressure; it becomes computational only when tied to an explicit measurable proxy.

The source's strongest public commitment is **semantic sovereignty**: people should be able to inspect, question, transform, and recompose interpretations applied to them. Symbolic readings remain editable interpretations rather than objective verdicts. Silence, listening, uncertainty labels, source separation, and contestability are safeguards against premature naming and symbolic overreach.

## Teaching potential

1. **Metaphor as lens, not verdict** — symbolic language proposes a view and must remain revisable.
2. **Semantic sovereignty** — visitors retain inspectable and contestable authority over applied interpretation.
3. **Field awareness** — teach context as relations, pressures, absences, and observer position rather than isolated objects.
4. **Bounded traversal** — use the ring as a metaphor for entering, moving through, closing, and learning from a process.
5. **Operational translation** — require every computational metaphor to name its measurable proxy and uncertainty.
6. **Silence before classification** — delay premature naming when evidence is weak or the field is ambiguous.
7. **Symbolic safety** — distinguish source, interpretation, confidence, and consequence.
