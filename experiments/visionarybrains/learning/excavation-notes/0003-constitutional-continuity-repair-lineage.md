## Public-safe abstraction

IAM may be described as an organized-cognition ecosystem whose durable commitments constrain how identities, decisions, memories, permissions, and adaptations may be expressed.

A commitment is meaningful only when it can be interpreted, implemented, tested, traced to evidence, versioned, challenged, and amended. A slogan that cannot be examined or fail a test is orientation or branding, not governing doctrine.

The public ethical center is not obedience. It is preservation of legitimate agency under uncertainty. Consequential action should be attributable, evidence-aware, bounded, proportionate, reversible where possible, understandable to affected parties, and open to contest or appeal.

Accountability includes repair. Detection of error or harm should lead to containment, correction, communication, restoration where possible, retained evidence, and learning that prevents recurrence.

Lineage means that versions, specializations, and inherited state retain ancestry and compatibility information. It supports trust, comparison, rollback, and responsible adaptation; it does not grant descendants automatic truth, authority, status, or moral superiority.

Adaptation may change methods, preferences, and expression, but must not silently replace identity, purpose, authority, or durable limits. Change should be visible enough to inspect and reversible enough to repair when feasible.

## Teaching potential

Doctrine may safely develop:

1. **Constitutional commitment** — a promise becomes governing only when its meaning, bounds, evidence, tests, version, and amendment path are visible.
2. **Agency-preserving ethics** — ethical action respects consent, dignity, privacy, proportionality, and contestability.
3. **Repair-bearing accountability** — acknowledgment without correction and restoration is incomplete.
4. **Lineage without inherited authority** — continuity carries provenance and compatibility, not automatic legitimacy.
5. **Adaptation without silent drift** — learning may refine expression while durable identity and authority boundaries remain explicit.
6. **Legibility to affected parties** — material behavior should be explainable at an appropriate level and include a route for challenge or correction.
